If You Need Police or Shelter Help

Step-by-step, in plain language. You don't need a parent's permission to ask for any of this.

🚓 Contacting the Police

  1. Call Police Emergency: 15 if you are in danger right now.
  2. For reporting abuse (not an emergency), you can call Madadgaar: 1098 or Child Protection: 1121 first — they can guide you or contact police on your behalf.
  3. To file a formal report yourself (an FIR), go to the police station covering the area where it happened. By law, the officer in charge must record your report — they cannot refuse.
  4. You'll tell them what happened in your own words; they'll write it down and read it back to you to confirm it's correct.
  5. You are legally entitled to a free copy of the report — ask for it before you leave.
  6. There is no fee for any of this, and you do not need a lawyer to make a report.

🏠 Finding a Shelter

  1. Call Child Protection: 1121, Madadgaar: 1098, or Sahil: 0800-13518 first — they can connect you with a safe shelter rather than you having to find one alone.
  2. Government shelters (Dar-ul-Aman) and organizations like the Edhi Foundation accept women and children in distress, including minors without a parent present.
  3. It's safest to reach a shelter through one of these helplines rather than traveling to one on your own — they can help arrange safe transport and make sure you're expected.

💬 No Trusted Adult, No Shelter Contact Yet?

  1. That's still okay. Start with a phone call — Madadgaar (1098) or Sahil (0800-13518) are built for exactly this, and you don't need anyone's permission to call.
  2. You can explain your situation and ask them directly what your options are — they can guide next steps, including shelter and legal support.
  3. If you're in immediate danger, call 15 directly. Police are required to respond.

🛡️ Cybersecurity Guide

Protecting Your Accounts

  • Use a different, strong password for every account — never reuse one.
  • Turn on two-factor authentication wherever it's offered.
  • Never share passwords or verification codes with anyone, even someone claiming to be a friend or "support."

Spotting Online Predators

  • Be cautious of adults or strangers who want to move conversations to private chats quickly.
  • Watch for anyone asking you to keep a relationship "secret" from your family — this is a common manipulation tactic, not a sign of trust.
  • Be wary of anyone pressuring you for photos, personal details, or meeting in person.

If You're Being Blackmailed or Threatened Online

  • Do not pay, and do not send more photos or information, even if threatened — this usually leads to more demands, not fewer.
  • Take screenshots of the threats before blocking the person, as evidence.
  • Report it to the NCCIA Cyber Crime Helpline: 1799 — this is exactly what they handle.
  • You are not in trouble for being targeted. The crime is what was done to you, not anything you did.
